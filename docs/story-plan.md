# The Rain Shop · 雨铺
A one-hour animated film. Ink-wash look, made entirely in code. Subtitles in English and 中文, no voices.

## In one line
A grumpy grandma sells bottled weather in an old water town. When a drought comes, her clumsy grandson opens the one jar she swore never to open.

## What makes it different from the last two films
- **Look:** Chinese ink-wash on rice paper (black ink, grey washes, paper grain) instead of soft 3D watercolor.
- **Color as story:** the town is ink-grey. The only bright colors are the weathers glowing inside the jars. When the rain finally returns, color spreads over the whole town for the first time.
- **Characters:** an old woman and a boy who don't get along, not a boy and his pet.
- **Story shape:** a family story in one town over one summer, not a trip across planets.
- **Music:** guzheng, dizi flute, erhu-like strings and real rain sounds, instead of bells and pads.

## Characters
- **Granny Yun (云婆婆), 70s.** Keeper of the Rain Shop. Sharp-tongued, proud, never says sorry. Catches weather in celadon jars with a bamboo ladle and seals them with red paper. Secretly very tender.
- **Xiaoman (小满), 9.** Her grandson, sent from the big city for the summer while his parents work. Clumsy and bored, but curious. Draws comics in a notebook. Wants to be useful and feels like he is in the way.
- **Old Ink (墨老).** The shop's elderly cormorant. Silent, dignified, always the one who gets splashed. The comic relief.
- **Boss Qian (钱老板).** A slick merchant from upriver with a big painted boat. Wants to buy all of Granny's weather to sell in the city: sunshine for tourists, snow for weddings. Funny, greedy, not evil.
- **The Rain Dragon (雨龙).** Revealed late. A young dragon made of rain, thin and frightened, who has been inside the red-sealed jar for sixty years.
- **Townsfolk:** a bride, a rice farmer, a tea-house poet, the twins who sell lotus seeds, and the ferryman.

## The spine
**What you hold on to too tightly cannot live.** Granny has spent her life keeping weather safe in jars. Xiaoman teaches her to let it go, and she teaches him that he belongs.

## Three acts, 13 scenes (about 60 min)

### Act 1: The shop (0:00 to 18:00)
1. **Plum Rain Morning (5 min).** Dawn, rain on black tiles. Granny catches raindrops from the eaves; in the ladle they gather into glowing threads and she pours them into jars. Labels: "Plum rain, day 3", "Thunder over Tiger Hill", "First frost". Customers come in: the bride wants a gentle snow for her summer wedding, the farmer buys a cup of drizzle for his seedlings, and the poet buys "autumn fog". It is magical and calm, and introduces the town.
2. **The Boy on the Boat (5 min).** Xiaoman arrives by ferry, sulky, with headphones that don't work here. Granny's welcome is a board with three rules: 1. Don't touch the jars. 2. Don't touch the jars. 3. NEVER the red one. Old Ink stares at him.
3. **The Giggling Breeze (4 min).** Xiaoman tries to help and knocks over a jar of "giggling breeze". Chaos follows: hats fly, the poet's pages swirl, and Old Ink tumbles across the room. Granny is furious. Xiaoman sits on the bridge drawing a comic of a monster grandma.
4. **The Red Jar (4 min).** At night, Xiaoman sees Granny talking softly to the red-sealed jar on the altar, by candlelight. He can't hear the words. The mystery is planted.

### Act 2: The drought (18:00 to 42:00)
5. **No More Rain (5 min).** Summer comes and the rain stops. In a time-lapse, the canals sink to cracked mud, boats are stuck and the lotus wilts. The town queues at the Rain Shop. Granny rations her jars and sells them cheaply, and the shelves slowly empty.
6. **Boss Qian (4 min).** A big painted boat comes as far as the water allows. Qian offers a chest of silver for everything. Granny throws him out with a broom. On his way out, Qian notices the red jar.
7. **Learning to Catch Weather (6 min).** Granny reluctantly teaches Xiaoman, and the two grow close. He catches morning mist on the bridge with a silk net, then a sunbeam in a teacup, then the cool of a firefly evening. He fails again and again. At last he catches one tiny cloud the size of a dumpling, which follows him everywhere like a puppy. Granny laughs for the first time.
8. **Sixty Years Ago (5 min).** One stormy night, Granny tells the story, shown as an ink painting in the style of shadow puppets. As a girl, during the great flood, she trapped the river's storm in the red jar to save the town. Since then the town's rain has come only from small passing clouds, and this year they have run out. She is afraid that opening the jar would bring the flood back.
9. **The Thief (4 min).** At night Qian steals the red jar to sell as "the biggest storm in the world". Xiaoman chases him with his dumpling cloud and Old Ink. In the struggle under the stone bridge the jar cracks, and a thin thread of mist escapes.

### Act 3: Letting go (42:00 to 60:00)
10. **The Chase (5 min).** Across dry canals, over rooftops and through the market, to Qian's boat stuck in the mud. The jar finally breaks. It is not a flood: it is a young dragon made of rain, thin, weak and scared. Xiaoman understands that the drought came because the dragon has been trapped. It needs to go home to the sky, but it is too weak to rise.
11. **Every Jar in the Shop (5 min).** The climax. Granny arrives. She sees the dragon and finally understands. The whole town carries out every jar left in the shop, her life's work, and they open them together: drizzle, snow, fog, the giggling breeze, thunder over Tiger Hill. All the weathers swirl in color and lift the dragon. Last of all, Granny opens her own secret jar ("the rain on the day your mother was born") and lets it go too.
12. **The Rain Returns (4 min).** The first real rain in months falls. Color spreads over the ink-grey town for the first time. The canals fill, children dance, and Qian gets soaked, laughs, and helps push boats.
13. **Autumn (4 min) + credits (2 min).** The shop sign now reads "雨铺 · Granny & Xiaoman". The shop no longer keeps weather; it lends it. Xiaoman leaves by ferry at the end of summer, and Granny, who never says sorry, hands him a small jar: "Yours. Open it when you miss me." In the city, on a grey day, he opens it: the sound of rain on the shop roof, and his dumpling cloud. He smiles at the window. Credits roll as ink paintings of the town.

## Look rules
- Rice-paper texture everywhere. Ink lines with brush pressure (thick to thin). Grey washes with soft bleeding edges. Lots of white space.
- Main palette: ink black, five greys, paper cream. Accents only: celadon green (jars), vermilion (seals, the red jar), soft indigo (night).
- Weather inside jars glows in color. After scene 12 the whole town is in soft color.
- Camera: slow horizontal pans like unrolling a hand scroll, and a few dramatic vertical moves (the dragon rising).
- Format: 2.39:1, 24 fps, like the last film.
